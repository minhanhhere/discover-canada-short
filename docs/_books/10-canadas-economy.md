<h3>A Trading Nation</h3>
<p>
  Canada has always been a trading nation and commerce remains the engine of economic growth. As
  Canadians, we could not maintain our standard of living without engaging in trade with other
  nations.
</p>
<p>
  In 1988, Canada enacted free trade with the United States. Mexico became a partner in 1994 in the
  broader North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA), with over 444&nbsp;million people and over
  $1&nbsp;trillion in merchandise trade in 2008.
</p>
<p>
  Today, Canada has one of the ten largest economies in the world and is part of the G8 group of
  leading industrialized countries with the United States, Germany, the United Kingdom, Italy, France,
  Japan and Russia.
</p>

<h3>Canada’s Economy Includes Three Main Types of Industries:</h3>
<ul>
  <li>
    <strong>Service industries</strong> provide thousands of different jobs in areas like
    transportation, education, health care, construction, banking, communications, retail services,
    tourism and government. More than 75% of working Canadians now have jobs in service industries.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Manufacturing industries</strong> make products to sell in Canada and around the world.
    Manufactured products include paper, high technology equipment, aerospace technology,
    automobiles, machinery, food, clothing and many other goods. Our largest international trading
    partner is the United States.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Natural resources industries</strong> include forestry, fishing, agriculture, mining and
    energy. These industries have played an important part in the country’s history and development.
    Today, the economy of many areas of the country still depends on developing natural resources,
    and a large percentage of Canada’s exports are natural resources commodities.
  </li>
</ul>

<p>
  Canada enjoys close relations with the United States and each is the other’s largest trading
  partner. Over three-quarters of Canadian exports are destined for the U.S.A. In fact we have the
  biggest bilateral trading relationship in the world. Integrated Canada-U.S.A. supply chains compete
  with the rest of the world. Canada exports billions of dollars worth of energy products, industrial
  goods, machinery, equipment, automotive, agricultural, fishing and forestry products, and consumer
  goods every year. Millions of Canadians and Americans cross every year and in safety what is
  traditionally known as “the world’s longest undefended border.”
</p>
<p>
  At Blaine in the State of Washington, the Peace Arch, inscribed with the words “children of a
  common mother” and “brethren dwelling together in unity,” symbolizes our close ties and common
  interests.
</p>
